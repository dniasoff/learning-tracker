Learning Tracker — Design Directions

Six visual approaches for the Today screen. All directions use the same design foundation (MD3, curriculum colors, RTL content, child/adult modes). They differ in layout philosophy, information density, and visual weight.

Design Philosophy

  • Maximum items visible without scrolling (6+ on screen)
  • Thin 3dp border accents — curriculum color is present but understated
  • No card elevation — borderless list rows for minimal visual noise
  • Inline progress bars — micro-sized, non-competing
  • Header: date + count + streak in one compact row
Best for: Adult mode default. Users who want to scan and tap through quickly. Scales well to 10+ daily items.

Design Philosophy

  • Each task is a distinct, elevated card — clear tap targets with 16dp corners
  • 4dp curriculum color border on each card
  • Personalized greeting header (child mode)
  • Achievement framing on progress ("7 done! 5 to go!")
  • ~4 items visible before scroll — prioritizes clarity over density
Best for: Child mode default. Users with fewer daily items (3-5). Makes each completion feel like an event.

Design Philosophy

  • Tasks grouped by curriculum with colored section headers
  • Circle checkbox (curriculum-colored border) instead of card border
  • Clear visual hierarchy: curriculum > task > stage
  • Great for users with 3+ curricula — each section is visually distinct
  • Downside: breaks scheduler priority ordering in favor of curriculum grouping
Best for: Heavy multi-curriculum users (4-5 curricula). Clear mental model of "what do I have for each seder." Trades scheduler ordering for organizational clarity.

Design Philosophy

  • Large progress ring dominates — every completion visibly fills the ring
  • Remaining/Completed split — completed items stay visible (greyed out)
  • Compact task rows below the hero — information dense once past the ring
  • Streak prominently placed inside the hero area
  • Downside: hero ring consumes ~40% of viewport — less room for tasks
Best for: Users motivated by visible progress. Child mode with fewer items. Creates strong "filling up" satisfaction as the ring completes.

Design Philosophy

  • One prominent task card — full detail, large text, generous padding
  • Queue below at 50% opacity — visible but not competing for attention
  • After completing the focus task, next queue item promotes to focus card
  • Minimal header — date and counter only, no stats or greeting
  • Downside: hides the full picture — user can't easily scan all 6 items
Best for: Users who feel overwhelmed by a full task list. Creates a "just do the next thing" mindset. Reduces decision fatigue.

Design Philosophy

  • Stats header: item count, streak, pace — at a glance
  • Curriculum chips show distribution before scrolling
  • Ultra-compact task rows — all 6 visible without scrolling
  • Stage badge on right side for quick scanning
  • Downside: information-dense header may overwhelm new or child users
Best for: Adult power users with 5+ curricula. Users who want the full picture at a glance. Data-forward, not emotion-forward.
